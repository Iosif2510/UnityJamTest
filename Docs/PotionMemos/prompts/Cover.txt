A flat scan of a real handwritten handover note that a young woman who runs a small apothecary shop wrote for a friend who will mind the shop while she steps out. It lies on top of her pile of working notes. It is handwritten and personal, NOT a designed document, NOT a printed form, NOT a poster, NOT an infographic.

Portrait sheet. Black pen only on plain white paper. No color, no gray wash, no paper tint, no desk or background around the sheet, no outer border, no printed lettering, no decorative ornaments.

HOW IT LOOKS:
- Unlike her other chaotic notes, she wrote this one CAREFULLY so that a stranger can follow it at a glance: the writing is larger and clearer than usual, well spaced, and the page reads top to bottom in clear blocks. It must be easy to read in ten seconds.
- Still obviously handwritten: lines drift slightly, sizes vary a little, underlines are wobbly and pressed twice, hand-drawn circles around the step numbers are lopsided.
- The title is BIG and bold, gone over twice.
- The two role blocks sit side by side under the title, each inside a rough hand-drawn wobbly loop (not a ruled box), with a tiny rough doodle on top of each.
- The four steps go down the page, each starting with a hand-drawn circled digit (1, 2, 3, 4). Under each step, its tip is written smaller, indented, starting with a little hand-drawn arrow.
- The last line at the bottom is the second most prominent thing on the page: large, with a hand-drawn wobbly loop around it and a star doodle next to it.
- All sketches are SMALL and VERY ROUGH doodles done in a few seconds, wobbly single outlines, no shading.
- Wear on the paper: one fold crease across the middle, one or two ink drops. Keep the paper otherwise clean.

HANDWRITING: all writing is in KOREAN, in the round, cute handwriting of a young woman, like a tidy page of a personal diary. Every Korean phrase below must appear, copied EXACTLY character for character, digits and punctuation included, clearly legible. Do not add any other words in any language.
